Have an Enemy

Sometimes the best way to know what your app should be is to know what it shouldn't be. Figure out your app's enemy and you'll shine a light on where you need to go. When we created Basecamp, we knew Microsoft Project was the gorilla. We used it as a motivator: Basecamp would be the anti-Project.

We realized project management isn't about charts, graphs, reports—it's about communication. It isn't about a manager broadcasting a plan; it's about everyone taking responsibility. Our enemy was the Project Management Dictators. We wanted to democratize it. Projects turn out better when everyone takes collective ownership.

When we built Writeboard, competitors had lots of whizbang features. We emphasized "no fuss"—share and collaborate simply, without non-essential features. If it wasn't essential, we left it out. Three months after launch, over 100,000 Writeboards had been created.

One bonus: a clear marketing message. People are stoked by conflict. They understand a product by comparing it to others. With a chosen enemy, you're feeding people a story they want to hear. They'll take sides—a sure-fire way to get attention. Don't get too obsessed with the competition. Overanalyze and you'll limit the way you think. Take a look, then move on to your own vision.

Don't follow the leader. Marketers are trained to follow the leader—outdo the competition on price or speed. The problem: once a consumer has bought someone else's story, persuading them to switch is persuading them to admit they were wrong. People hate that. Instead, tell a different story. If your competition is faster, you must be cheaper. If they sell health, you sell convenience. A real story completely different from the one already being told. —Seth Godin

Stay focused on the big picture: What is the key problem we are trying to solve, and how can we solve it?
