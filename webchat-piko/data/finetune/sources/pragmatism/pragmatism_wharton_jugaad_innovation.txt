Jugaad: A Frugal, Flexible Approach to Innovation

Jugaad is a Hindi word meaning "an improvised solution born from ingenuity." The six principles of jugaad: Seek opportunity in adversity, do more with less, think and act flexibly, keep it simple, include the margin, and follow your heart.

Ben Franklin exemplified jugaad in early America. He learned frugality—doing more with less. The Franklin stove used less wood and produced more heat. He never patented his inventions; he practiced "include the margin," making inventions beneficial for the larger community. He was a serial entrepreneur before the term existed.

The Western innovation engine has become too rigid, insular and bloated. After World War II, companies fell in love with industrializing everything to achieve economies of scale. Predictability became sacrosanct. But when the world changed faster, that predictability became an impediment to being agile. The world's top 1,000 R&D investors spent $550 billion in 2010 but didn't get much return. The alternative: frugal, flexible, inclusive innovation—jugaad.

Jugaad is an innovative fix under severe resource constraints. It's about being flexible in the face of adversity and frugal in the face of scarcity. Example: Mansukh Prajapati in the Indian desert created Mitticool—a refrigerator that doesn't use electricity, using clay's natural cooling. Two compartments: water flows down, evaporates, cools the lower chamber. No grid, green, biodegradable.

Do more with less: KPIT Cummins created Revolo, a low-cost plug-in conversion kit to turn gas cars into hybrids in six hours for about $2,000. One engineer stuck in Mumbai traffic came up with the idea. It increases fuel efficiency 35% and reduces emissions 35%.

Simplicity: Philips embraces simplicity as a design tenet. Customers are put off by complexity. Facebook's interface is very simple—social design puts the user experience first, technology takes a back seat. Siemens, known for engineering prowess, now asks: can we come up with good-enough devices that are simple to use and maintain?

Include the margin: P&G developed a low-cost line for the first time in 38 years. Walmart offers check cashing in stores for the unbanked. Sixty million unbanked or underbanked citizens in the US; 50 million without health insurance. Tap marginal segments—they're growing.

Advice: Pick one principle to begin with, e.g. doing more with less. Apply it in one area. Leadership matters—jugaad is about transforming culture and mindset. Start small; drive cultural transformation with the right incentives and metrics.
