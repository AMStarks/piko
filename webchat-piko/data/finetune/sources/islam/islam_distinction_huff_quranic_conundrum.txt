If the Qur'ān is true, then it's false – The Qur'anic Conundrum

Many Muslims make objections to the Christian faith in the form of accusations against the reliability, preservation, and trustworthiness of the Bible. However, the Qur'an itself argues for the preservation of the Christian scriptures.

1. The Qur'ān routinely refers to the "previous Scriptures," identified as the Torah (Tawrat, mentioned 18 times) and the Gospel (Injil, mentioned 12 times). These are "sent down by God" (e.g. Surah 3:3, 5:68). "Say, O People of the Scripture, you are on nothing until you uphold the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to you from your Lord."

2. Muhammad is told in Surah 10:94 that if he has doubt he should ask those who have been reading the Scripture before him: "The truth has certainly come to you from your Lord, so never be among the doubters."

3. The Qur'ān declares that Allah's words cannot be changed (6:114-115): "None can alter His words, and He is the Hearing, the Knowing."

4. Christians in Surah 5:46-47 are told to judge by the Gospel; if they do not, they are "defiantly disobedient": "Let the People of the Gospel judge by what Allah has revealed therein."

Conclusion: If the Torah and the Gospel are corrupt, as modern Muslims claim, then the author of the Qur'an seems to have no knowledge of it—the Qur'an articulates their trustworthiness. If these Scriptures are God's word and no one can change God's word, then how have they become corrupted? Did Allah not know they would be corrupted (not all-knowing)? Could he not stop it (not all-powerful)? Or is 6:115 incorrect (Qur'an compromised)? Why would Muhammad be encouraged to talk to people with corrupt Scriptures (10:94)? We know what the Torah and Gospel looked like in Muhammad's lifetime; manuscripts from that period are virtually identical to today's. If the Qur'an's commands had application for their original audience, what was "the Gospel" being discussed? Why would Allah tell Christians to judge by the Gospel if it had been corrupted?

If I take the Qur'ān at its word as a person of the Gospel and judge the Qur'ān by the Gospel in accordance with 5:47, I find it wanting. I see no engagement with what the Gospel says. I see contradictions and misunderstandings. If the Qur'an is true and I obey its command to me, then I have to conclude that it is false.
