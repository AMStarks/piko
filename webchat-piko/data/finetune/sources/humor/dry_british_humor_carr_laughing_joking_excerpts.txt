Jimmy Carr – Laughing and Joking – Excerpts (deadpan, British wit)

I've been described as the hardest-working man in comedy. Not that impressive, is it? The hardest-working man in comedy. That's like being the best-looking guy in the burns unit. No offence to any burns victims we've got in. Are there any in? If there's one, there'll be fucking loads. They tend to stick together.

Someone came up to me outside and went, "I thought you'd be younger." I said, "I was."

The average person in Great Britain laughs out loud ten times a day. Not everyone, obviously. If you work in a hospice or with learning-disabled adults, it could be ten times that. The sky's the limit!

I was at a show, watching a band, standing just under the fire escape with a pint and a mate. A lady from the venue came up and went, "Excuse me, you're going to have to move. Because, if there's a fire, you're blocking the exit." I said, "I tell you what, love. If there's a fire, I'll move." What did she think I was going to do? Just stand there going, "Nobody move! Why has everything gone orangey and hot?"

I was checking into a hotel at one in the morning. The guy recognised me and went, "Oh, Mr Carr, we've put you in a disabled room." I said, "What's the difference between a normal room and a disabled room?" He said, "The disabled room's got a walk-in shower." That's taking the piss, isn't it?

Does anyone believe in the supernatural? Ghosts and spirits? It's actually easy to tell if your house is haunted. It isn't. Grow up.

I was outside a nightclub and discovered that women can be bouncers if you're travelling fast enough when you mount the pavement.

I was in a hotel having breakfast and the waiter said, "Do you want white or brown toast?" I said, "All toast is brown. You're thinking of bread."

At any one time, a bowl of nuts on a bar will have 17 different types of urine on them. And that's why they're called peanuts.

I got invited to Clarence House, where Charles and Camilla live. We're all in her front room—the most ornate space, every square inch of mantelpiece has a beautiful object. She gets to me and goes, "Oh, how are you involved?" I went, "Never mind about that. Have you seen Cash In The Attic? Cos we're sitting on a gold mine here." To her credit, she went, "Yes," winked and fucked off. Brilliant.

At Buckingham Palace, an armed police officer asked me, "Are you an al-Qaeda?" I went, "No." "As you were." He told me a story: The Queen went to Glasgow to open a drop-in centre for homeless alcoholics. She asked a Scottish, alcoholic, homeless man, "What do you do?" He came back with, "Same as you—nothing!" Apparently she was terrified.
