Biblical Critical Theory – Key Quotes

C.S. Lewis: "Oh, that's easy. It's grace." The notion of God's love coming to us free of charge, no strings attached, seems to go against every instinct of humanity. The Buddhist eight-fold path, the Hindu doctrine of karma, the Jewish covenant, and Muslim code of law—each offers a way to earn approval. Only Christianity dares to make God's love unconditional.

James K. A. Smith: "It's precisely when your ultimate conviction is that there is no eternal that you're most prone to absolutize the temporal."

Chesterton: "The modern world is full of the old Christian virtues gone mad" because they are "isolated from each other and wandering alone." Some scientists care for truth; their truth is pitiless. Some humanitarians only care for pity; their pity is often untruthful.

Jacques Ellul: "Let us not hitch Christianity to whatever political cart happens to be passing. Our grandchildren will not thank us for it."

If I am a Christian, those who disagree with me are not by default intellectually inferior or rationally benighted. They have every chance of being my betters. If I claim to be a Christian and do not acknowledge that my "enemies" are very possibly my moral and intellectual superiors, I have failed to understand the gospel. I am still operating according to the performance narrative, not the grace narrative.

"I am a democrat because I believe in the Fall of Man. I think most people are democrats for the opposite reason." The danger of defending democracy on the grounds that mankind is wise and good is that it's not true.

We should maintain what C.S. Lewis calls "scrupulous care to preserve the Christian message as something distinct from one's own ideas."

To live in the last days is to live in overlaps and tensions: the "now" and "not yet," the "in" and "not of," giving to Caesar what is Caesar's and to God what is God's—within a culture that thinks it is rejecting Christianity at the very moment it affirms its debt to Christian figures.
